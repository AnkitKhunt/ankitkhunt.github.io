01 ABOUT

RESEARCHER PROFILE

Exploring Einstein’s Gravity in the Strongest Regimes of the Universe.

I am Ankit Khunt, a relativistic astrophysicist whose work centres on understanding gravity in its strongest regimes. I earned my Ph.D. in Theoretical Astrophysics from Sardar Patel University, India, in 2024, with training in theoretical and computational astrophysics. Since completing my Ph.D., I have been associated with a college as a Visiting Faculty member in Physics.

My research asks how gravity behaves where spacetime is strongly curved and matter reaches extreme densities. I study neutron stars and black holes as laboratories for General Relativity and its extensions, combining semi-analytical relativistic modelling with numerical calculations to connect theory with astrophysical observations.

A major focus is the structure and stability of neutron stars: how pressure anisotropy changes mass, radius, and stability; how dark matter may alter stellar interiors in two-fluid models; and how modified gravity can affect observable properties. I am interested in gravitational waves, quasi-periodic oscillations (QPOs), tidal response, orbital dynamics, and post-merger signals as ways to test these models.

At the black-hole end, I am interested in near-horizon physics and gravitational-wave echoes as possible probes of departures from classical horizon behaviour. More broadly, I am motivated by the way mathematical structure in relativity links matter, space, and time—and by how observations can challenge that structure.

02 RESEARCH DIRECTIONS

Exploring gravity, matter, and signals in the most extreme astrophysical environments.

My research connects general relativity, compact-object physics, and computational astrophysics to investigate how extreme gravity shapes the structure, stability, dynamics, and observable signatures of neutron stars, black holes, and other relativistic systems.

R01 / COMPACT OBJECTS ✳

Neutron Stars & Dense Matter

Relativistic stellar structure, equations of state, mass–radius relations, compactness, surface gravity, redshift, and dense nuclear matter. My work includes geometrically motivated core–envelope models, anisotropic stars, and dark-matter-admixed neutron stars.

TOV equations Dense matter Core–envelope Dark matter
R02 / GRAVITY ◉

Strong-Field Gravity & Spacetime

Testing general relativity in extreme gravitational environments and exploring extensions of gravity, including Starobinsky-type f(R) gravity, energy–momentum-squared gravity, and Einstein–Cartan theory. Related interests include spacetime curvature, wormholes, black-hole formation, and particle and light trajectories.

General relativity Modified gravity Wormholes Black holes
R03 / STABILITY & DYNAMICS ⌁

Stellar Stability & Oscillations

Investigating compact-star stability through anisotropic pressure, sound-speed constraints, cracking analysis, and radial and non-radial oscillations. Further interests include relativistic asteroseismology, quasi-periodic oscillations (QPOs), and orbital and epicyclic frequencies in compact-object spacetimes.

Cracking stability Asteroseismology QPOs Orbital dynamics
R04 / MULTIMESSENGER SIGNALS ≈

Gravitational Waves & Astrophysical Signals

Connecting theoretical models with electromagnetic and gravitational-wave observations to constrain dense matter and gravity. Research interests include tidal deformability, moment of inertia, compact-binary mergers, post-merger signals, quasi-normal modes, gravitational-wave echoes, pulsar observations, and accretion phenomena.

Gravitational waves Tidal response Post-merger signals Multimessenger
